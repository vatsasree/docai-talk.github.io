Part 1 · The vocabulary, precisely

Agents and tools

A tool

deterministic

Fixed function. Same input, same output.

A detector cannot notice that it merged two columns, or that the “table” it found is really a form.

detectors · OCR · table structure
reading order · font fitting · background restoration

An agent

reasons

Decides which tool, when — and whether the result is good enough.

It can retry, reroute, or override what it was given.

labelling · grouping · routing
specialist generation · reading order · reflection
The goal: the rendered page matches the source — not that the markup looks plausible.
REPLICAWhy agentic
Part 1 · Four questions, four stages

The framework, stage by stage

1Segment“How do we divide the page into meaningful pieces?”
  • A classifier decides digital or scanned; a detector ensemble proposes candidate regions.
  • A labelling agent marks each region and catches mis-segmented or mixed content.
  • A grouping agent resolves caption–figure binding, list nesting and footnotes.
  • → a hierarchical layout tree
2Localize“How do we turn each piece into HTML?”
  • A router agent picks the specialist from the crop itself — overriding the upstream label when the visual contradicts it.
  • Six specialists: text, table, image, form, list, equation.
  • OCR and table-structure tools are called only when needed.
  • → sub-HTML per region · independent, so this stage parallelises
3Assemble“How do the pieces fit together?”
  • A position-aware merge walks the tree bottom-up, preserving containment and alignment.
  • A reading-order agent linearises semantically — sorting top-to-bottom fails on multi-column pages.
  • → the first complete page · reading-order accuracy 0.98
4Refine“Does it actually look right?”
  • Font and background tools fit non-overflowing type and restore the page's backdrop.
  • A reflection agent renders the page, compares it to the source image, and injects targeted CSS — up to five rounds.
  • → the final page · the only stage that sees the render

Part 1 · Reading the scoreboard

Which metric measures what

Four axes, one metric family each. Two of the four had no metric at all before this work.

AxisThe question it asksMetricHow it is computed — in one line
Text ExtractionAre the words right? WRR · CRR standard Word- and character-level recognition rate against the reference text.
Logical StructureIs the hierarchy right? NTED · LSS standard Tree-edit distance between the produced DOM and the reference tree — reading order, nesting, tables, lists.
Physical StructureIs the position right? GPS · LPS NEW Area overlap between where each element sits in the source and where it lands in the render — GPS over layout blocks, LPS over individual lines.
Visual FidelityDoes it look right? VFS NEW A VLM judges the two rendered pages side by side on typography, colour, spacing and layout.
OverallHow good, all in? OSThe mean of the four axes — so a system cannot buy a good score by winning one axis.
Position and appearance are scored by where things land and what the page looks like — never by inspecting the markup, which is exactly how earlier benchmarks missed them.

Limitations

Single-page only

No multi-page consistency yet — structures spanning a page break, such as continued tables, can fragment.

Font identification

Bold, italic, underline and colour are recovered reliably; exact font families are not. Large-scale multilingual font recognition is still open.

Rendering cost

The refine loop renders in a browser repeatedly — real latency against a single forward pass.

Paper 1Pipeline vs end-to-end
Paper 1 · Credit where it is due

The plug comes from LISA. Everything around it is new.

LISA · Lai et al., CVPR 2024

Reasoning segmentation on natural images.

One token → one mask. Trained end to end with text + mask losses. Followed by GLaMM, PixelLM, LISA++ — all on natural scenes.

What documents forced us to add

Transfer fails. Pages are text-dense and far from natural scenes — these models “transfer poorly to documents”. So: a document data engine, 2M grounded QA, no hand-drawn masks.
Evidence is plural. One answer, K pointers — K chosen by the model.
Evidence has scope. Three nested masks per pointer: phrase ⊂ line ⊂ block.
Text has priors. Don’t bleed off the text; finer masks nest in coarser ones — as losses.
…and in the next two papers: pointers that agree across steps (GIM), preference training that keeps the masks (anchor), and a page that is a tree (HGC).

Paper 1 · Training

Two document-specific losses

Don’t spill off the text

a bleed-suppression loss penalizes every bit of mask that lands off the text

The three masks must nest

an enclosure loss makes phrase ⊂ line ⊂ block — the coarser mask is the finer one’s ground truth

Both are trained jointly with the usual text + segmentation losses — together they are worth +5 F1 on grounding.

Why preference training?

Sample the SFT model twice on the same question — both answers are “160 ≠ 150”:

chain A — right, and shows it

S₁ the Net Sales column [GND] → the column ✓
S₂ values 20 · 20 · 20 · 50 · 30 · 20 [GND] → six cells ✓
S₃ they sum to 160 — printed total 150 [GND] → the total ✓

chain B — right answer, broken trail

S₁ the Gross Sales column [GND] → wrong column ✗
S₂ the values total 160 [GND] → mask on the logo ✗
S₃ …so the answer is no — right, for the wrong reasons

SFT never shows the model two of its own chains and says which is better. Preference training is exactly that comparison.

Paper 2DoCoG-PQA · why preferences
Paper 2 · Training stage 2 · background

From preferences to DPO, in four lines

1 · a preference

p(yw ≻ yl | x) = σ( r(x, yw) − r(x, yl) )

Bradley–Terry: a hidden reward r explains which of two outputs people prefer

2 · RLHF

maxπ 𝔼[ rφ(x, y) ] − β · KL( πθ ‖ πref )

fit a reward model, then push the policy toward it without drifting far from the SFT model — needs RL, often unstable

3 · the optimum is closed-form

π* ∝ πref · er/β  ⇒  r = β log π*/πref + const

the reward can be read off the policy itself

4 · substitute — the constant cancels

𝓛DPO = −𝔼 log σ( β log πθ(yw)/πref(yw) − β log πθ(yl)/πref(yl) )

a classification loss on the policy — no reward model, no RL

For a token sequence, log π is a sum of next-token log-probabilities — the loss touches exactly what the language-model head writes.

Preference training, on the text

𝓛DPO = −log σ( β [
log πθ(chain A | I, q) πref(chain A | I, q)
the better chain — pushed up
−
log πθ(chain B | I, q) πref(chain B | I, q)
the worse chain — pushed down
] )

π = probability of writing that chain

token by token — steps, words, and the [GND] tokens. DPO shapes where the model grounds, though it never sees a mask.

So only the VLM moves

every term is text → the mask pipeline appears nowhere → ❄ frozen.

Why no mask term?

a preference needs a likelihood — the decoder emits one mask, not a distribution.

The catch — this leashes the text. Nothing leashes the hidden states the frozen mask pipeline reads.
